Do tool words carry their body part beyond word similarity, under a stronger test?

Designed and run by an AI (Claude) for AE Studio's Fractal team on 19 November 2026, 13:49 UTC.

Answer

Yes: a probe on Qwen3-8B hidden states names a held-out tool's body part at 0.93 vs 0.79 on GloVe (+0.14, CI 0.09 to 0.19); against fastText vectors (0.86) the edge shrinks to +0.07 (CI 0.03 to 0.11).

Mid-layer states of Qwen3-8B carry more of a tool's using body part than static word vectors do, but part of the GloVe gap is GloVe's weakness: a better word vector closes about half of it.

The follow-up I chose to run: Retest whether Qwen3-8B hidden states know which body part uses a tool beyond plain word similarity: train a cross-validated linear probe on hidden states (layer picked on held-out nouns) versus the same probe on GloVe, and check whether hidden states add accuracy over GloVe alone. Unlike the earlier run, which used cosine to two anchor words, use a cleaner and larger tool list (main WordNet sense must be a tool or instrument, dropping non-tools like boss and story) so the gap with GloVe is measured more tightly than the earlier -0.11 to +0.12.

My earlier run in this thread answered: No: at the chosen layer (31), Qwen3-8B's hidden states put 0.615 of held-out tool words nearest the body part that uses them. GloVe word vectors score about the same (0.611); chance is 0.33. What it ran: Model: Qwen3-8B. The tool nouns come from the Lancaster Sensorimotor Norms. Each was kept only if WordNet places it under artifact and not under food, it has a GloVe vector, and it contains no body-part string. Each noun was labelled hand/arm, foot/leg or mouth by its strongest human action rating, which had to be at least 2.5 and at least 1.0 above the other two. There are 60 nouns per class, split into 90 selection nouns and 90 reporting nouns. For each noun, the last-token hidden state at all 36 layers was read under 12 neutral carrier sentences (3 disjoint sets of 4, used as seeds). The states were centred per carrier and layer, then averaged within each set. Each noun was assigned to the body-part class whose vector (the mean of two anchor words) had the highest cosine. The selection half chose layer 31 (mean accuracy 0.652). On the 90 reporting nouns the hidden state scored 0.578, 0.633 and 0.633 across the three sets (mean 0.615). GloVe scored 0.611 and was the stronger baseline; the model's input embeddings scored 0.378. The paired bootstrap 95% interval on hidden-state minus GloVe accuracy runs from -0.111 to 0.119. The positive control (24 words for parts of each body part) scored 0.79 to 0.83, at least 0.18 above the tool-word baseline. Shuffled-label chance was 0.33, with p = 0.001 for each set. On the 35 reporting nouns GloVe gets wrong, the hidden state got 0.43 to 0.49 right. After removing GloVe similarity, the partial Spearman correlation between hidden-state similarity and the human ratings averaged about 0.03. The plan's 'counts against' condition is met: the hidden state is well above chance but no better than plain word similarity. None; this is a readout only. Main condition: centred last-token hidden states at the chosen layer. Controls: the same readout on the model's input embeddings, on GloVe vectors, with shuffled labels, and on words for parts of each body part.
